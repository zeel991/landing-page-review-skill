# Archetypes from the 32 reviews

The 32 pages are condensed here as product archetypes, not named, because pages change and a verdict from October 2026 should not anchor a review today. Use these to calibrate a score and to find the closest comparable for the page in front of you.

## Scored 10/10 or "best of the day"

**Developer tool for giving coding agents controlled access to a chain.**
Hero animation showed the app honestly without pretending to be the real app. Micro-animations carried meaning: hovering a provider actually revoked it, proving the granular control the copy promised. Story as you scroll: one visual of the tangled state, then one of the simplified state. Snappy. Naming the chain was fine because the buyer was a developer. "Intent, true design"; "technical stuff deserves beauty as well".

**Coding education for kids.**
One-line proposition. Full-page hero kept quiet, with rotating examples of what kids built to keep it alive. Trust for parents in the hero itself: "trusted by N parents", "no card needed". Short CTA text on the button, longer version beside it. Clickable video stages. Answered the obvious objection on the page. Many FAQs were tolerated because that audience is anxious. No chain mentioned, none needed.

**Consumer payments app for one country.**
Strong two-word domain. Hero told him what it was instantly. Playful and clean. A "made in <city>" badge, normally an AI-tell, worked as founder-led storytelling. Let visitors claim a handle, which starts building the user base before launch. A story as you scroll, a 30-second version, the card and perks. Only open question was differentiation; the real job left was distribution.

**AI trading companion with a mascot.**
Hero copy straight to the point. Mascot humanised the agent for people who still think of AI as a chat box, and it followed you down the page. One CTA: download. Visuals alternating left and right. Never mentioned the chain. Only nit: contrast too low for accessibility and search.

**Sports prediction markets.**
"A lot of messaging done with few copy, highly visual." He was not the audience and still understood 75% in seconds. The page was strong enough to have "earned the right" to ask for an email on a waitlist. Shipped a brand book. Fixes: a second product on the same domain diluted the brand; prune the nav while in waitlist mode.

**Euro-denominated DeFi yield.**
Full-page hero did the design work while the copy stayed to one line plus a two-word CTA. The differentiator (a non-dollar currency) was the hero. Yields visible before any wallet connection. Docs answered the next question. A currency toggle was a nice touch. Association with a regional accelerator read as legitimacy.

**Savings baskets for emerging markets.**
Simple proposition, example portfolio, a compounding calculator ("$50 a week for 20 years") that sells better than any paragraph, risk profiles, encouraging copy. Fixes: remove "connect your wallet" and "mainnet" for a non-native audience; could add one subtle background motion.

**Prediction markets inside live streams.**
Real design effort. A small demo explained the product; quick links to live markets so people could act immediately. Fixes: a "live" pill that was an AI tell; palette too close to a well-known social app. Next step was partners, not the page.

## Strong, with specific fixes (7–9)

**Private payments tooling.** Strong tagline, hover-revealed animation, SDK and agents guide for credibility. AI-made but "they've removed the slop". Fixes: a button that did nothing; mock demos to replace with real ones; say whether it is a protocol, an app, or a SaaS, and how it differs from the framework it resembles.

**Tokenised index vaults.** Full-page hero, one-line proposition, app one click away, humanised team with bios, audited, public repo, SDK. Fixes: show the best yield in the hero; "RWA" in copy but only US stocks shown, which is not new; decide retail vs institutional; the same three partner logos repeated.

**Tokenised stocks app (pre-launch).** Honest copy, 60/40 hero, many demos, a real privacy policy. Fixes: "early access" pill duplicating the waitlist; two CTAs where the page already shows the app; "see how it could work" undersells; auto-advancing demo not clickable; FAQ down to six; drop filler sections. "A good eight, a nine with less info, a ten with a tasteful scroll story."

**Accountability tracker.** Beautiful background motion, clean demo of the real app, simple pricing cards, three-word value props. Fixes: four text levels in the hero; "try the app" beside "early access"; an "illustrative data" pill; a dark-mode toggle instead of following the system; dozens of exposed languages the copy was never checked in. "Remove 20% of the copy and you'd be on your way."

**Marketplace for ad space on personal items.** Hero so clear he understood it before scrolling; the image explained the rest. Fixes: image too tall on a 14-inch laptop; two sections repeating each other; example sellers who were not on the platform.

**Mobile game.** For a game the page only has to make it look fun within 30–45 seconds; real footage did it. Could cut half the content.

**Market intelligence for sophisticated traders (returning winner).** Dense and fancy was acceptable because the buyer is sophisticated and the product was visibly mature: live feed without login, good wallet selection, steady blog cadence. Fixes: scroll-hijacking animation that looked like the end of the page; sign up, sign in and community could collapse into one CTA.

**Compliance screening for AI agents.** Perfect hero copy of the "<job> for <new actor>" shape. Clean, pitch-deck-like. Fixes: too much information architecture; move depth to docs and add a sitemap and llms.txt for agent discovery; say why it beats the incumbents.

**Crowdfunding for robots.** Great copy, beautiful animations, a pleasure to scroll. Fixes: 3D hero lagged on a top-end laptop and ignored reduced-motion; em dash in hero; three CTAs plus a chat link; CTAs that went to a social profile or back to the top; blinking pills; show a featured raise.

## Clear product, messaging not landed (5–7)

**Fleet records SaaS.** Did not name the chain (praised). Five words in the hero. Fixes: audience still unclear; a "shop" link that sells nothing; no pricing; no screenshot; prune the nav to the one CTA because funnelling to the founder's inbox is the page's only job; a gimmicky AI-generated illustration.

**Tokenised stocks (crowded niche).** Elegant, no scroll needed, instantly understood. Fixes: no differentiator at all; "I don't care if you're on five chains"; three pieces of waitlist information; a waitlist button that did nothing. "I'm close to giving you my email, but you need to give me that last thing."

**Revenue layer for trading agents.** Beautiful branding, a machine-readable toggle. Fixes: hero copy far too long and listing table stakes; four sections where one would do; secondary CTA to remove; a win-rate claim with no trades or P&L behind it.

**Attention-based prediction markets.** Good catchphrase. Fixes: cards with a pointer cursor that only refreshed the page ("a lie, already you're losing confidence"); a fake "live" label; login before any market was shown; put a working widget in the hero and log people in after; tiny text; a "docs" link that was a how-it-works.

**Creator commerce suite.** The CTA verb was the clearest thing on the page. Fixes: could not tell which incumbent it resembled; a fee that looked high for crypto; stock-photo person; language toggle causing layout shift; half-translated blog; "coming soon" tools; audience unclear.

**On-chain neobank.** Classic clean pitch. Fixes: the name collided with a famous fintech (legal risk, rename); "try the beta" beside "join the waitlist" with no explanation; a "live in beta" pill and an em dash; a language picker that could not change the language.

**Legal agreements on chain.** Great domain. Fixes: two CTAs with no guidance; two chains with no reason; ambiguous core term; when a web2 category moves on chain, the page must say why; a bilingual toggle that confuses a global audience.

**Gig marketplace for humans and agents.** Nice team section. Fixes: unslop; a marketplace needs a CTA per side in that side's words. Macro warning: marketplaces are a fifth of submissions, dominated by incumbents, and everyone builds agent-payment infrastructure while nobody builds the thing users touch.

**Market data collectors for quants.** The CTA was the only clear thing; the hero was jargon. Fixes: a CTA on every scroll; docs as a button instead of a link; primary CTA left, sign-in secondary; broken images; published pricing for institutions is pointless, collapse to pay-as-you-go plus professional and add "contact us"; a misspelled foreign word in the brand.

**Stock-and-token pairing pools.** Live, sortable data; working command palette. Fixes: a private-bank look for a speculative product with no reason; no stated difference from the incumbent; search without typo tolerance; a half-AI wizard ("either drop the wizard or make it a full LLM, anything in between is not appreciated"); dark mode ignoring the system.

**Physical-goods provenance chips.** Simple layout, nice animation, well-made video, team visible. Fixes: far too much explanatory text, move it to FAQ or docs; put the demo video in the hero, max 30 seconds, skip the logo intro; hero copy down to five to ten words. "Good design will not save you from bad copy."

## Told to start over on messaging (below 5)

**AI red-teaming for security teams.** Beautiful, but "prompted by a security researcher, not a go-to-market person". The buyer already knows what red-teaming is, so: cut the header copy in half, cut the next block in half, drop the stats and the AI-looking panel, and offer exactly two things: the sample report and a 30-minute booking link.

**Five-minute up/down prediction market.** Liked that markets showed immediately and that there was a skeleton loader. Fixes: every AI telltale (extra labels, "live now", meaningless pills), cards inside cards, sidebar overload, unrounded fractions of a cent, missing thousands separators, database-precision timestamps. No differentiator in a crowded narrative. Advice: stop building, polish two features, give it to ten friends.

**Token and stock data aggregator.** Half dashboard, half landing page; weekly findings belong in a report email. Fixes: one chart showing the one thing nobody else has; decide whether it is an API, a dump, or a report; say who it is for and where they click to pay; unreadable font sizes; no docs.

**Email enrichment suite.** Landed straight in a dashboard of identical tools with identical copy and a login wall before anything ran. "You're showing me that you do a lot, but you're showing me none of it." Start with one tool, do it well, explain it simply, then expand.
