# The rulebook, with evidence

Every rule below was said in at least two of the 32 reviews unless marked as a single-site lesson. Quotes are near-verbatim from the transcripts. Read this when a review needs backing, or when you are deciding which of two fixes matters more.

## 1. The hero is a five-second test

- "The copy of the hero is the thing that matters the most. Get this down to five to ten words max." (veymark)
- "Your message should be a few words. It should be visible within a few seconds what you're trying to sell me." (appliedwill)
- "In the past six months the meta has changed. You need to get people understanding your product right away without me having to read." (krexa)
- "Good design will not save you from bad copy." (veymark) "Now with LLMs, a good design is not enough. Clarity of messaging in the era where people are overwhelmed with text, because text is cheap." (veymark)
- Hero text has at most two levels. "There's four levels in your hero. Get that down to two." (operatoruplift)
- The hero carries the differentiator when the niche is crowded (see rule 5).
- Praised hero copy: "AML and fraud prevention for AI agents" (tennto), "Private money on public chains" (gloam), "Trade what's trending" (motionmkts), "The DeFi layer for European stablecoins. Deposit euros." (kurate), "Capital for machines that move" (robocap), "AI and coding education for kids" (learnspace), "Sent to any handle in seconds" (paymentsorted), "One click your own ETF with yield" (coffer).
- Full-page heroes are fine and often praised, as long as the message stays short and the hero stays "quiet" (kurate, coffer, learnspace).

## 2. Unslop: the AI telltales he removes every time

He calls it "unslop", "AI telltale signs", "telltale AI". Each of these cost a page points:

- Status pills and badges: "live now", "live in beta", "early access", "waitlist open", "mainnet", "illustrative data", "native". Remove them. The waitlist button already says it. (playhunch, trymercury, stature, anystock, operatoruplift, sproot, motionmkts, robocap)
- Blinking or pulsing dots and "live" indicators on things that are not live. "This is not live. So that's a lie. Already you're losing confidence." (motionmkts, robocap, oddie)
- Em dashes in copy. (trymercury, robocap)
- Three or four stacked text levels in the hero; a subtext under every card title. "I don't need to know 'a rhythm you can return to' and the other subtext." (operatoruplift)
- Cards inside cards with identical styling; many sections with the same layout. "This card should be a top level one but it has the same styling as the one above it." (playhunch, gtmmaxing)
- Sections that repeat each other: "Workflow" and "Artworks" (adspaces), "How it works" plus key value props (stature), product + harness + track record + company (krexa).
- Tiny body text nobody reads. "Even my cat here is not trying to read it." (motionmkts, stonkpile)
- "Coming soon" items. "Remove them. Just launch what you have." (lid, playhunch)
- Language pickers that don't work or only half-translate. (trymercury, lid, soft.law)
- Stats and trust numbers nobody asked for. "The rest of your stats, I'm not interested." (appliedwill)
- Phonetic pronunciations, decorative jargon, and French for flavour. (aetelier)
- Fancy animation that blocks scrolling or looks like the end of the page. (conyr)
- The "look at me, I'm scientific" density. "Guilty of way too much fancy stuff." (conyr)
- General rule of thumb he gives: "I'd remove 20% of the copy and you'd be on your way." (operatoruplift) "Cut the header copy by half. Cut this by half too." (appliedwill) "Maybe you could cut half of that." (inkfall)
- Why: "AI is bad at showing information quickly for people. An LLM can chew up a million tokens for cheap; humans spend their time on TikTok and have five seconds of attention." (tennto) Fancy design no longer signals effort: "Everybody has good animations now because models are good at it." (wurk) "I'm not as impressed by those as six months ago." (krexa)

## 3. One primary call to action

- "Two call to actions are rarely something that helps." (soft.law) "Why do I have two buttons? Are they the same thing?" (operatoruplift)
- Never offer "try the app" and "join the waitlist" together. If the app is usable, no waitlist; if not, waitlist only. (operatoruplift, trymercury, stature)
- Docs are a nav link, not a button. Sign-in is a secondary button. Primary CTA sits left. (aetelier, krexa)
- Do not repeat the CTA at every scroll. "Every time I scroll they're like click me, click me again." (aetelier)
- Prune the nav down to the one action the page exists for. "Remove all of these links. Just keep the 'request pilot' CTA. That's really the only thing this website is built to do." (smartfleetsync, kickoff)
- Sign up, sign in and community links can collapse into one button with a modal. (conyr)
- Marketplaces and launchpads need one CTA per side, written in that side's words: "earn money on the side" / "browse jobs" vs "simplify your life"; "launch a robot" vs "back the machines". (wurk, robocap)
- CTA copy on the button can be the short form, with the long form next to it. (learnspace)
- The CTA should go where it says. "Back the machines takes me to Twitter." "Launch your robot takes me back to the top." (robocap, anystock)

## 4. Show before you ask

The single most repeated principle. His framing: "Trust is earned, attention is a currency. Don't ask users to connect, show the possibilities first."

- Never gate the first look behind a wallet connect, email, or login. "Not interested in giving you my info until I trust you, my friend." (motionmkts) "I still like to not ask people their email or their wallet before they can actually do an action." (kickoff) "Why would I log in and connect my Gmail?" (gtmmaxing)
- Show the thing people came for: the markets, the yields, the vaults, the live feed, the pools, the example portfolio. (oddie, kurate, playhunch, conyr, restock, sproot, coffer)
- Prefer a working widget in the hero over a screenshot: "Let people click, let people put the amount of the position, and then log them in." (motionmkts)
- A demo video belongs in the hero, max 30 seconds, skip the logo intro, sound off is fine. (veymark)
- Demos should be navigable by the user, not auto-advance too fast. (stature, learnspace)
- Mock demos are acceptable if honest ("not the real app, but it tells me enough", lanatools) and are replaced with real ones as soon as they exist (gloam).
- Anything that looks clickable must do something. Cursor pointer plus page refresh is a lie. (motionmkts, anystock, gloam, robocap)
- The exception he grants: a page that is exemplary and clearly pre-launch "has earned the right to ask for an email". (kickoff)

## 5. Say how you are different

- In every crowded niche he asks the same question: "How is it different?" Prediction markets, tokenized stocks, trading agents, neobanks, privacy, marketplaces, AML. (playhunch, anystock, restock, krexa, trymercury, gloam, tennto, wurk)
- Table stakes are not copy: "Every fill settles on chain. I'm kind of expecting it if you're an on-chain agent." (krexa) "I don't care if you're on five chains." (anystock)
- A claim needs proof: "87% win rate. Give me the trades, give me the P&L. Is it real?" (krexa)
- When a web2 category is moved on chain, the page must say why. "I love to tell people don't mention it's on chain unless there's a need. But if something already exists in web2 and you think it's better on chain, you need to explain why." (soft.law)
- Two chains, two products, or two brands on one page need a reason or they dilute the brand. (soft.law, kickoff)

## 6. Know the audience and write only for them

- Consumer products: humanize, be friendly, no chain words. "Remove 'mainnet'. People don't know what mainnet means." (sproot) "This does not even mention on-chain or Solana. This is important." (getomen) "I love businesses who use Solana the technology but don't feel the need to say it out loud." (smartfleetsync)
- Technical and security buyers: fewer words, give the artifacts. "Give me the report. Give me a 30-minute call so I can book you." (appliedwill) SOC 2, audits, public GitHub, SDK, docs, agents guide all count. (coffer, gloam, conyr)
- Developer and LLM-native audiences: naming Solana is fine, and technical pages still deserve beauty. (lanatools, conyr)
- Institutions never self-serve: "An institution will never look at your prices and say 'yeah that's what I'm going to pay'. They will call you. Multi-year procurement. You never show a price for an institution." Put "contact us" and keep two simple tiers for everyone else. (aetelier)
- Match the look to the market. A degen speculation product dressed like a private bank raises the question "why". (restock)
- When reviewing outside your audience, say so, and still report what you understood in a few seconds. (kickoff, gloam, coffer, inkfall)

## 7. Trust signals that work

- Team with faces and clickable bios: "Six people shipping fast. A lot of the time you don't know who to trust." (coffer, wurk, lanatools, veymark)
- Founder-led storytelling: "Made in Brisbane", who built it. "Use your founder as your first marketer." (paymentsorted)
- Audits, SOC 2, privacy policy, public repo, SDK, docs. (coffer, appliedwill, stature, gloam)
- Association with a known entity (Superteam chapter, previous Colosseum winner). (kurate, conyr, tennto)
- Social proof that fits the audience: "trusted by 200 parents", "no card needed", examples of what users made. (learnspace)
- A blog with a steady cadence (a post every few weeks) builds search authority over months; it is passive distribution, not a landing page section. (conyr)
- A brand book, in HTML rather than PDF, so partners can grab the logo without a call. (kickoff)
- Polish details are trust: round money to cents, use thousands separators, show the user's currency, round timestamps to the minute, make search tolerate a typo. "Small things, but they accumulate. It makes the difference between a polished product that earned my trust or one that hasn't." (playhunch, restock)
- Honesty in copy is praised ("we're building a simple app"), but don't undersell: "see how it could work" becomes "see how it works". (stature)

## 8. Product and positioning signals he reacts to

- Half dashboard, half landing page is a confusion engine; a landing page sells one thing. (stonkpile, gtmmaxing)
- Kitchen-sink suites: "Start with one tool, do it well, expand to others." (gtmmaxing)
- Weekly data findings belong in a report email, not the hero. (stonkpile)
- Marketplaces: "always 20% of submissions", dominated by incumbents, you must build the supply side, and examples of sellers who are not on your platform don't count. (wurk, adspaces, robocap)
- Infrastructure for agents (x402 etc.) is over-built; products that use it invisibly are what he is bullish on. (wurk)
- Name collisions with established companies are a legal risk; rename. (trymercury)
- Multiple products under one domain: establish one first. (kickoff)
- A half-AI wizard is worse than no wizard: "either drop the wizard or make it a full LLM". (restock)
- Mascots humanize AI agents for people who still think "chat box". (getomen)
- Games only need to look fun within 30–45 seconds; real footage does it. (inkfall)
- Calculators and example portfolios sell better than paragraphs (compound interest tool). (sproot)
- Risk profiles and encouragement copy for savings products. (sproot)
- Vault and yield products should show the best yield in the hero. (coffer, kurate)

## 9. Performance, accessibility, responsiveness, localization

- Heavy 3D or particle heroes lag "even on a MacBook M5 with 128 GB"; turn it down and honour `prefers-reduced-motion`. (robocap)
- Contrast too low gets penalized by Lighthouse/SEO. (getomen)
- No layout shift on toggles. (lid)
- Dark mode follows the system; a manual toggle is optional, a toggle that ignores the system is wrong. (operatoruplift, restock)
- Animation must not hijack scroll or fake a page end. (conyr)
- Hero images sized for a 14-inch laptop: the CTA must be visible without scrolling on desktop. Always check mobile too. (adspaces)
- Localization: either English only, or five or six major languages fully translated. A bilingual toggle confuses a global audience, and half-translated blogs are worse than none. Short copy is what survives Thai, Russian, and German expansion. (soft.law, lid, operatoruplift)
- Page speed is noticed: "extremely snappy, super fast" is praise; "a little bit slow" is a mark against. (lanatools, coffer)
- For agent discovery, use a sitemap and llms.txt instead of stuffing the page with text. (tennto)

## 10. What he praises as "intent, true design"

- Micro-animations that carry meaning (hover revokes an integration; a cursor that blinks like a terminal). "Micro animations are always thoughtful." (lanatools)
- Story as you scroll: problem state, then your simplified state; a mascot or element that follows you. (lanatools, getomen, stature, paymentsorted)
- Simplicity with one subtle background motion to keep people scrolling, "nothing fancy, nothing big". (sproot, stature)
- Pricing cards with two or three plain tiers. (operatoruplift, aetelier)
- Five or six FAQs max, more only when the audience is anxious (parents). (stature, learnspace)
- Domains: one word is best, two is fine; a TLD that fits the brand (.fun, .money, .law) is a plus. (paymentsorted, sproot, soft.law, adspaces)

## 11. How he delivers a review

- Raw first impressions, narrated while scrolling: "So what is it?" then reads the hero aloud and says what he thinks the product is.
- States the five-second read, then the first scroll, then clicks every CTA to see if it works, then checks docs, blog, team, pricing, dark mode, mobile.
- Separates design from messaging and judges messaging first. Admits when he is not the target audience and does not judge the tech.
- Gives a score out of ten when the page is decent, and names the one or two changes that would move it a point.
- Praises specifically (the exact section or detail) and criticises specifically (the exact pill, button, or sentence).
- Ends with a recap of three to five fixes in priority order: hero copy, CTA, clutter, trust, details.
- Tone: direct, warm, a little blunt ("my friend", "unslop it"), never cruel; "I'm not trying to be mean here."
